How it works

  • Two buckets, because Australia has two. Super is untouchable until preservation age 60; everything outside it can be sold at any time. Retire before 60 and the years in between have to be paid for entirely from outside super — the bridge. That single rule is what makes an Australian FIRE number different from an American one.
  • Real tax, not an average rate. Income is run through the resident rate scale with the Medicare levy, LITO and — once you are 67 — SAPTO. Employer and sacrificed contributions are taxed at 15% going in, with Division 293 adding another 15% above $250,000. Excess concessional contributions are added back to your income with a 15% offset, the way the ATO does it. Fill the cap makes the sacrifice a standing instruction instead of an amount, recalculated every year — employer super grows faster than the cap, so the room left for a sacrifice shrinks as you go.
  • Super earnings are taxed at 15% while you are still accumulating, and at nothing once the balance is supporting a retirement-phase pension after 60 — which also switches on the minimum drawdown, 4% of the balance rising to 14% by 95.
  • Outside super, the yield and the growth are taxed differently. The distribution yield is taxed as income every year and reinvested; franking credits are applied as refundable offsets. The rest of the return is capital growth, untaxed until you sell — and when you do, the gain is discounted by 50% and taxed at your marginal rate, which in retirement is often nothing at all.
  • The age pension is estimated from both tests — assets, and income by deeming — and you get the lower of the two. The home you live in is excluded. It is indexed with inflation, so it holds its value in today's money.
  • Money is simulated in actual dollars and every threshold, cap, pension rate and spending figure is indexed to inflation, so the Today's money view is a straight deflation of the same numbers. Growth is credited on the opening balance plus half of the year's flows.
  • The range of outcomes chart draws a fresh return for each year of each of 1,000 runs from a normal distribution with your expected return and volatility, then re-runs the whole plan — tax, pension and drawdown included. The seed is fixed, so the same inputs always give the same fan. If super and outside super are given different expected returns, both are moved by the same draw each year — one market, two allocations — so a bad year is bad in both places at once.

What it does not do

  • Your home, your mortgage and its offset account; investment property, negative gearing and land tax; HECS/HELP; private health cover and the surcharge; insurance premiums inside super.
  • Non-concessional contributions and the bring-forward rule, downsizer and spouse contributions, the government co-contribution, LISTO, and the carry-forward of unused concessional cap.
  • The transfer balance cap ($2.1M a person) and the tax on the excess; the proposed extra tax on very large balances.
  • The Work Bonus, rent assistance, the pension loans scheme, and the difference in age pension rates between illness-separated couples.
  • Anything about you: your job security, your health, your family, or the chance that you change your mind at 48.
